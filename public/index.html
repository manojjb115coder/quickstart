Unit 5: Presentation on Course-Related Topics – Becoming a Reflective Information Scientist 

In this unit, we had presentations on different topics related to our course. One presentation that interested me was “The Turing Test: Can Machines Imitate Humans?” The main question discussed was, “Can machines actually think?” The presentation was about Alan Turing and the Turing Test. As we know, “thinking” cannot be clearly defined. Therefore, in 1950, Alan Turing came up with a game known as the “Imitation Game.” The main aim of the “Imitation Game” was to find out who among the two participants was actually a machine and who is human. Therefore, in the “Imitation Game,” a human judge tries to determine who out of the two chats is actually a machine and the other a human being. If the judge cannot find which one is the machine’s text then the machine is passed. So the presentation was focused on how AI and machines imitate human feelings, emotions and conversation style.

Unit3 Reflection

Unit 3 Reflection: Learning From Failure

My perspective on failure changed completely after completing Unit 3. I thought that failure is a mistake and it should be avoided. Therefore,I did not see failure as something useful, instead I saw it as a mistake . After the completion of unit 3, I felt that failure can actually help us to learn many things, if we understand it properly.

Then our group worked on a failure case study, which was the ‘Boeing 737 Max’ case. First I got shocked as I did not expect that such a big failure could happen in a well developed aircraft system. From my viewpoint, this example proves that despite of technological development, overconfidence and ignorance of minor problems may result in significant issues as one single sensor did all the mistakes in this ‘Boeing 737 Max’ case. I am sure that the reason why the accident occurred was not only associated with the malfunction but also because of unawareness of pilots.
