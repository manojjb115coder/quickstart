## My Learning Experience

## Unit 1 Reflection – Understanding What Information Truly Means

In my introductory class, I initially thought that learning this course would be simple, since it involves studying the basics of information, data, technology, and tools related to information systems. However, after a week of classes, I realised that information is actually a deeper concept. Information looks simple at first, but it becomes complicated when we try to define it clearly or when I try to work with it in practice.

At the beginning of the unit, in my first class, I went through the course overview explained by my instructor. This helped me understand what the course is trying to achieve. Later, I realised that the course focuses on thinking critically about information, data, and how they are used to solve problems. One important thing I learned is that information science is not only about collecting data but also about understanding the meaning that can be derived from that data.

After the course overview, I learned about wikis and Markdown, which were completely new concepts for me. I had never heard about these tools before. Because of this, working with Markdown and writing mathematical expressions in Zettlr initially felt unfamiliar. I usually write notes using normal document editors, so using Markdown for the first time required some adjustment.However after practice I came to know that writing in markdown encourages clearer organization of headings, lists, and links, which then helps structure ideas more effectively.

Another important part of the unit was reading the book The Information: A History, a Theory, a Flood by James Gleick. I was assigned some chapters to read as part of my group work. My task was to help create a learning resource based on those chapters. The assignment involved writing a blurb, chapter summaries, and observations. At first, I did not know what a “blurb” meant, so I took help from my groupmates to understand it better. Writing a blurb required summarising the main idea of the author in just one or two sentences while still making it interesting for the reader. I found this challenging because it required identifying the most important idea quickly. After that, I wrote chapter summaries of around 150–200 words. I avoided including my personal opinion and instead focused on explaining what the author was trying to say. This helped me understand the difference between summarising and interpreting information.The final part of the task was writing observations and comments about the chapters. This part felt easier for me because I was able to express my thoughts about what I learned from the reading.

Later in the unit, I learned about the DIKW model (Data, Information, Knowledge, Wisdom). This model explains how raw data can be transformed into meaningful knowledge. Data represents raw facts, while information is organised data that has context. Knowledge emerges when we understand patterns within the information, and wisdom involves making better decisions using knowledge. Although the model appears simple, it helped me understand that interpretation and context are very important when moving from data to knowledge and wisdom.

Overall, Unit 1 changed the way I think about information. I now have a better understanding of the difference between data and information, and I also learned about tools such as wikis, Zettlr, GitHub, and Git Bash. These experiences helped me to realise that working with information is not just about collecting data, but also about organising, interpreting, and communicating it effectively.
