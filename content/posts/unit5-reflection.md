**Unit 5: Presentation on Course-Related Topics – Becoming a Reflective Information Scientist** 

In this unit, we had presentations on different topics related to our course. One presentation that interested me was “The Turing Test: Can Machines Imitate Humans?”
The main question discussed was, “Can machines actually think?”
The presentation was about Alan Turing and the Turing Test. As we know, “thinking” cannot be clearly defined. Therefore, in 1950, Alan Turing came up with a game known as the "Imitation Game." The main aim of the "Imitation Game" was to find out who among the two participants was actually a machine and who is human. Therefore, in the "Imitation Game," a human judge tries to determine who out of the two chats is actually a machine and the other a human being. If the judge cannot find which one is the machine's text then the machine is passed. So the presentation was focused on how AI and machines imitate human feelings, emotions and conversation style.  

The presentation also included one example of comparison between old chatbot (ELIZA) with new chatbot (GPT-4). This shows how Artificial intelligence progressed over time.However the presentation also mentioned some limitations of the Turing test such as the conversations are usually very short, so it becomes difficult to judge which one is the machine as human judgment can also be confusing because if the conversation sounds natural, people may believe that the machine is human. The presentation also conveys that AI can only  copy or imitate human language and behavior, but imitation does not mean that it is really thinking or understanding. A machine can also answer correctly without considering emotions and feelings. 

The group concluded that even though AI may think as human but AI becomes more good at communicating and behaving like a human in conversation. Therefore, the Turing test is relevant and significant even in modern times since it allows one to measure how much advancement has been made in AI development. In sum, the presentation was an effective and brief overview of the Turing test and encouraged individuals to think about intelligence and human behavior.
